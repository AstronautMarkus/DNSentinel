Overview

What is DNSentinel?

DNSentinel is a simple yet powerful web application designed with a single, focused purpose: automatically keep your dynamic IP address synchronized with your DNS server, especially when your Internet Service Provider (ISP) frequently changes your IP address.

Audience

Who is it for?

Perfect for users who manage services at home or in small offices with dynamic IP addresses, meaning their ISP changes their IP address periodically, often without them even noticing. DNSentinel ensures their services and websites remain accessible without interruptions, even when their IP changes.

The problem with having a dynamic IP (not static) is that your IP address can change without warning, which may cause interruptions in access to your services and websites. For most users who do not have a static IP provided by their provider, solving this can be impossible or very expensive, limiting the available options.

How it works

Your DNS follows your IP

DNSentinel automatically updates your router's IP address in the background, ensuring your DNS always points to the correct IP. This way, your services and websites remain accessible regardless of any IP changes made by your Internet provider.

Automatic IP updates

Detects when your public IP changes and updates your Cloudflare records in the background.

Runs on your network

DNSentinel runs only in your local network and talks directly to the Cloudflare API.

Reduced downtime

Your services and websites stay reachable, even right after your ISP changes your IP.

Easy setup

Connect a zone with its Zone ID and an API token, then import your existing records.

Before you start

Is DNSentinel right for your connection?

DNSentinel solves one specific problem: a public IP address that changes. Updating DNS only helps if visitors from the Internet can actually reach that IP, so check which of these cases matches your connection before you set it up.

Works

Dynamic public IP

Your router gets a real public IP from your ISP, and that IP changes from time to time. Port forwarding works, but your DNS records go stale after every change. This is exactly the case DNSentinel is built for.

What to do: connect your Cloudflare zone and import the records that point to your home IP.

Won't work

Behind CG-NAT

With Carrier-Grade NAT, your ISP shares one public IP among many customers and your router only gets a private address. DNSentinel would detect that shared IP and write it to your records, but incoming connections stop at your ISP's NAT and never reach your network. Your sites stay unreachable no matter how up to date your DNS is.

How to tell: look at the WAN IP on your router's status page. If it doesn't match your public IP, especially if it's in the 100.64.x.x to 100.127.x.x range, you are most likely behind CG-NAT. Ask your ISP for a public IP, or use a tunnel instead.

Not needed

Using a tunnel

Cloudflare Tunnel, ngrok, Tailscale Funnel and similar tools open an outbound connection from your server, and your DNS points to the tunnel (for example a CNAME to <id>.cfargotunnel.com), not to your IP. Your IP can change and nothing breaks, so there is nothing for DNSentinel to update.

What to do: keep the tunnel and leave its records out of DNSentinel. Replacing the tunnel's CNAME with an A record would send traffic around the tunnel.
